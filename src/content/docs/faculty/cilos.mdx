**Manage CILOs** holds the intended learning outcomes for each Course you are
assigned to.

## What a CILO is

A **Course Intended Learning Outcome** is what one Course is meant to produce. It
is narrower than a Graduate Outcome and narrower still than an Institutional
Learning Outcome — a CILO belongs to a Course, not to a person or a program.

## Choose a Course

CILOs are always scoped to one Course assignment. Open **Manage CILOs** and
choose the Course from the list. If a Course is not in the list, you are not
assigned to it.

## Create a CILO

1. Select **New CILOs** and choose the Course.
2. Write the outcome as something a Student can demonstrate by the end of the
   Course. Start with a verb.
3. Save.

Keep them observable. "Understand data ethics" is hard to evaluate;
"justify an ethical data decision in writing" is not.

:::caution
Write the CILO **before** you build the evaluation, not after. Questions bind to
CILOs, and a CILO added late cannot retroactively carry the evidence of a
question written for something else.
:::

## Edit a CILO

Open it and revise the wording. Editing the text does not disturb alignments or
published evaluations that already reference it — those keep the version they
were built against.

## Archive or restore

Archiving retires a CILO from new alignment work while keeping it readable in
historical periods, labelled **(Archived)**. Restore returns it to active status.

:::caution
Archiving is not the same as deleting, and it matters. A CILO removed while an
evaluation is running does not disappear from that evaluation's evidence. Use
archive for a topic you genuinely stopped teaching; correct the wording if you
simply wrote it badly.
:::

## Course ownership

CILOs belong to the **Course**, not to you personally. If a colleague takes over
your assignment, they see and maintain the CILOs you authored — and any question
bound to them.
