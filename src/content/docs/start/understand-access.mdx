System CLOIE checks permission on the server for every request. Nothing you can
do in the address bar widens what you can reach.

## Your role

Your role decides which workspaces and pages exist for you at all. A Student
account simply has no route to Course assignments — there is nothing to deny.

## Your scope

Within a page, your role is narrowed further by scope:

- A **Program Head** sees only the programs they are an authorized Program Head for.
- A **Faculty Member** sees only the Courses they hold an active assignment for.
- A **Dean** sees the whole college.
- A **Secretary** sees the whole institution, minus the areas the Secretary does not own.

## Viewing something does not always mean you can edit it

This is the most common source of confusion. A page can be fully visible to you
and still read-only.

| You can see | Who maintains it |
| --- | --- |
| CILO-to-outcome alignment | Faculty author the mappings; the Program Head reviews them |
| Institutional Learning Outcomes | General Education Coordinator |
| Graduate Outcomes | Program Head |
| CILOs | Faculty of the assigned Course |
| Course assignments (Secretary) | Program Head, General Education Coordinator, or Dean — read-only for the Secretary |
| Response details | Program Head sees identified responses; Faculty and Dean see anonymized responses only |

Prefer the phrasing "you can review these mappings, but Faculty maintains them"
over "mappings are available here".

## Access denied

An **access denied** or **unauthorized** message means your active role does not
authorize that route. It is not a broken link and not a loading failure.

Common reasons, in the order they are worth checking:

1. Your active role is not the role that owns the page.
2. Your scope does not include that program or Course.
3. Your profile gate is not satisfied yet.
4. The feature belongs to a role that has not been implemented for your account yet.

See [I cannot access this page](/troubleshooting/access-denied/).
